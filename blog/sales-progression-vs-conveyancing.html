Sales Progression vs Conveyancing: What's the Difference?

5 min read

The two get used almost interchangeably outside the industry, and confused fairly often inside it. They're not the same job. Conveyancing is the legal work that transfers ownership of a property. Sales progression is the active management of everyone and everything involved in getting a sale to that point, the conveyancing included.

Conveyancing: the legal transfer of ownership

A conveyancer or solicitor handles the legal side of a property transaction: drafting and reviewing contracts, running local authority and other searches, raising and answering enquiries between the buyer's and seller's solicitors, checking title, and handling the exchange and completion process itself. It's a defined, regulated piece of legal work with a clear start (instruction) and end (completion).

Sales progression: keeping the whole transaction moving

Sales progression sits above conveyancing and works alongside it. A sales progressor tracks the entire chain, every linked transaction rather than just one, chases every party (solicitors, mortgage brokers, surveyors, other agents) for updates and next steps, flags problems and key dates before they become urgent, and keeps vendors and buyers informed along the way. Conveyancing is the legal engine. Progression is the person making sure that engine, and everyone connected to it, keeps moving.

Why the confusion matters practically: an agency that assumes "the solicitors have it" is assuming conveyancing alone will keep a sale on track. It won't. Solicitors are, rightly, focused on their own client's transaction, and nobody's watching the other four links in the chain. That gap is exactly what a dedicated progression function exists to close. Where this sits in your agency

Some agencies run progression entirely in-house, with a dedicated progressor or team who owns the caseload from sale agreed to completion. Others outsource it to a specialist provider. Both are legitimate models. The right one depends on your volume, your margins and how much you want progression to feel like a direct extension of your own service.
