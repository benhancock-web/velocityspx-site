In-House vs Outsourced Sales Progression: Which Is Right for Your Agency?

6 min read

Both models work. Plenty of agencies progress every sale in-house, and plenty use a specialist outsourced provider, sometimes because they tried building it in-house and it didn't scale, sometimes the other way round. The right choice depends less on which is "better" and more on what you're actually trying to buy: control, or capacity.

Keeping it in-house

An in-house progressor is a direct extension of your agency. Vendors and buyers deal with someone wearing your branding, who knows your local market, your negotiators and often the specific vendor personally. You keep the full client relationship end to end, and any change to how progression is run, whether that's priorities, tone or escalation, is a decision you make directly rather than a conversation with a third party.

The trade-off is capacity and resilience. A single in-house progressor with a growing caseload has a hard ceiling: there's only so much active chasing and chain-tracking one person can do well. And when they're on holiday or off sick, real cover for that caseload is genuinely hard to arrange internally without dropping the ball on live cases.

Outsourcing it

An outsourced sales progression provider gives you capacity without hiring. Cases are picked up by a team, not one person, so there's built-in cover for holiday and sickness, and it can flex up during busy periods without a recruitment process. For a smaller agency, it can also mean access to progression expertise and process that would take time to build from scratch.

The trade-off is a layer between you and the client relationship, and less direct control over exactly how a case is handled day to day. Some vendors and buyers won't notice or mind. Some will, particularly at the higher end of the market where personal relationship matters more.

The real question isn't which model. It's what's actually limiting you today. If it's headcount and cover, outsourcing solves that quickly. If it's that progression feels disconnected from your negotiators and your brand, in-house, done properly with the right system behind it, solves that instead.

The case for making in-house work properly

A lot of the reasons agencies drift toward outsourcing, capacity, visibility, cover, are actually tooling problems as much as headcount problems. An in-house progressor working from a spreadsheet and a shared inbox will hit a ceiling fast. The same progressor with full chain visibility, automated chasing and a system that surfaces what needs attention today can carry meaningfully more cases. Cover gets easier to arrange too, once the whole caseload is visible to whoever's stepping in, not just what's in one person's head.
